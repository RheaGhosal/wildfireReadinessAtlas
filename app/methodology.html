Wildfire Readiness Atlas

Methodology and sources

This page explains what the Atlas shows, how its state comparisons are calculated, and what it does not claim.

← Return to the Atlas

Purpose

Wildfire Readiness Atlas is an educational dashboard. It helps students, families, and communities explore long-term wildfire-risk context and connect it with preparedness questions. It is not a fire prediction system, an evacuation map, or emergency guidance.

How state scores are calculated

Each state score is a population-weighted average of FEMA National Risk Index county wildfire relative-risk scores. For every county, its wildfire relative-risk score is multiplied by FEMA's reported county population. Those products are added within the state and divided by the state's total reported county population.

This gives counties proportional influence based on the number of people represented in the FEMA source data. The score is a comparative, long-term context measure—not a forecast of where the next wildfire will occur. County-level maps remain available so visitors can explore local variation rather than relying only on a state aggregate.

Coverage

This release includes the 50 U.S. states and the District of Columbia. U.S. territories are excluded from the state comparison in this release; they are not treated as low risk or as zero values. They will be considered only after the same source, boundary, and review standard is applied.

Sources used in the live site

Long-term wildfire-risk context
FEMA National Risk Index, version 1.20, county table, December 2025 release. Downloaded August 26, 2026. Fields used: county, state, population, wildfire relative-risk score, and wildfire expected annual loss.
County boundaries
U.S. Census Bureau 2024 cartographic boundary files, simplified for browser display.
Live satellite observations
NASA FIRMS heat detections. Detections are refreshed in the dashboard and are not confirmed fire perimeters or evacuation orders.
Live alerts
National Weather Service active-alert feed. Visitors should always follow local officials and emergency management during an active event.

Limitations

Risk measures do not capture every lived condition that can shape preparedness, including language access, disability access, housing, transportation, local communication networks, and access to clean indoor air. The Atlas is designed to help visitors ask those additional questions.
